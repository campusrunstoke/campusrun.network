Good Company

Sponsored lunches that fill a retirement home with college students for an afternoon. A brand picks up the tab and gets in the room. Everybody leaves stoked.

The idea in one line

A brand buys lunch, a sorority brings the energy, and a retirement home fills up with people for an afternoon.

Who gets stoked

Seniors get company. The one thing people in a retirement home actually want is someone to talk to. A room full of college students for an hour is the whole point.
Students get a cause. Greek life is always chasing volunteer hours and fundraising. This is a ready-made afternoon that counts, with lunch covered.
The brand gets its people. A brand like Sweetgreen puts product straight into the hands of its exact customer, the on-campus student, tied to a genuinely good thing instead of a folding table in a hallway.
Campus Run makes it go. We line up the sponsor, the chapter, the home, and the follow-up, so one call sets the whole thing in motion.

How one comes together

  1. A brand sponsors the lunch and brings its product for the day.
  2. We bring a chapter that needs the volunteer and fundraising hours.
  3. Everyone meets at a local retirement home for an afternoon of food and conversation.
  4. The brand gets the story and the numbers: who came, who opted in, and what it drove.

Why it works

Anyone can buy a table at a career fair and hand out samples. Good Company is different because three groups walk away happy at the same time. The seniors get company, the students get a cause, and the brand gets to be the reason it happened. That is worth more than a handout, and it is the part people remember. Everybody leaves with something, which is the whole point of stoke.

Want to run one? A first Good Company is one brand, one chapter, one home, one afternoon. We handle the rest, and you see for yourself what a room like that feels like.
